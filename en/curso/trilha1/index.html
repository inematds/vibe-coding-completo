🧠 The Vibe Coding Mindset

The shift: building by describing the result in natural language. Determinism vs. non-determinism, “trust but verify,” iterating in layers, AI as a learning tool, and who does what (you manage, AI develops).

Module progress 0% · 0 of 7
What it is:

Build software and automations by talking with AI in natural language—you describe the result, and AI handles the implementation.

Why learn:

Removes the technical overhead; focus on the problem, not the mechanics of each node. Speed and rapid iteration.

Key concepts:

Natural language drives the build; AI does the “heavy lifting”; just ask to iterate.

What it is:

Say what should happen (“identify technical questions”) instead of dictating how (“use an IF node with regex”).

Why learn:

Let the AI choose the best approach; you don't need to know the nodes.

Key concepts:

Outcome > instruction; specify the output; let the AI propose the structure.

What it is:

Traditional automation is predictable; generative AI is nondeterministic—the same instruction can generate different names, structures, and layouts.

Why learn:

Prevents confusing normal variation with an error; you don't compare screen by screen.

Key concepts:

"Boring is beautiful" in traditional automation; variation is expected in AI; focus on the result.

What it is:

AI makes mistakes — always test what was created and check the output against what you expected, even when there’s no apparent error.

Why learn:

A “green” workflow may be wrong; verification is part of the work.

Key concepts:

Trust and verify; green ≠ correct; test as a real user.

What it is:

Improve in steps (stair-step): make one improvement at a time, testing after each change.

Why learn:

Isolated changes are easy to test, revert, and diagnose.

Key concepts:

Layers; increment; measure before and after; back up before changing.

What it is:

Ask the AI to explain why it chose a node or configuration so the knowledge sticks.

Why learn:

You move beyond “set it and forget it” and develop an intuition for the system.

Key concepts:

Ask why; watch the build early; learn as you build.

What it is:

Treat AI as a specialist: you define the goal and approve; it executes, like an experienced developer.

Why learn:

Dividing roles prevents micromanagement and frees the AI to solve problems.

Key concepts:

Manager vs. developer; delegate; give feedback instead of corrections.

1.2 ~40 min · 6 topics

🧩 The Ecosystem: How the Pieces Fit Together

The lay of the land: the coding assistant, n8n (visual builder), MCP (external tools), skills & workflows, agents, and the difference between running locally and being hosted.

Module progress 0% · 0 of 6
What it is:

The engine that turns your conversation into actions: reads project files, writes/edits code, runs commands, and connects tools via MCP.

Why learn:

It's the centerpiece — understanding what it does (and doesn't do) guides everything.

Key concepts:

Runs in the editor; four capabilities; one folder per project.

What it is:

A visual, node-based workflow builder that connects apps, data, AI, and external APIs.

Why learn:

It’s where automations “live”; the assistant builds in it for you.

Key concepts:

Nodes; trigger → logic → action; visual and low/no-code.

What it is:

The protocol (Model Context Protocol) that exposes a set of external tools for the agent to use on its own.

Why learn:

It's how AI gets "arms" — connecting to n8n, scraping the web, reading databases.

Key concepts:

One connection, many tools; the agent decides when to use each one.

What it is:

Markdown files with reusable instructions — an “SOP” the agent follows, callable on demand.

Why learn:

They stop you from re-explaining tasks and provide consistency while saving context.

Key concepts:

Workflow = instruction; skill = callable workflow; markdown.

What it is:

A system that reasons, adapts, and decides the steps on its own based on the result you asked for.

Why learn:

It’s the leap from “wire everything by hand” to “describe the result.”

Key concepts:

Reasons and adapts; describes the result; non-deterministic.

What it is:

Run on your machine (you trigger it) vs. run on its own in the cloud (on a schedule or in response to an event, without you present).

Why learn:

Defines the course arc: starts locally and ends in production.

Key concepts:

Manual vs. automatic trigger; scale; the “agentic gap.”

📖 Essential Glossary — Part 1 (AI, Claude Code, n8n)

Seven term clusters defined with a concrete example: AI concepts, assistant modes, key files, MCP, basic n8n, n8n with AI, and data (vector store, embeddings).

Module progress 0% · 0 of 7
What it is:

The core vocabulary: LLM, prompt, system prompt, context window, token, and context rot.

Why learn:

Without these terms, everything else becomes orphaned jargon.

Key concepts:

Each term with a concrete example; context window; cost per token.

What it is:

The modes and commands: plan mode, bypass permissions, permission modes, /clear e /compact.

Why learn:

Control how much the AI does on its own and how you manage the session.

Key concepts:

Plan before acting; clear vs. compact; balance speed and safety.

What it is:

The files that structure a project: CLAUDE.md, .env, .gitignore, .mcp.json, and SKILL.md.

Why learn:

Knowing everyone’s role keeps you from leaking secrets and losing configuration.

Key concepts:

System prompt; secrets; what goes into the repository and what doesn’t.

What it is:

MCP terms: the protocol, the MCP server, the tool, and server discovery.

Why learn:

It’s how you give the agent new capabilities.

Key concepts:

Protocol; server; tool; where to find servers.

What it is:

The n8n building blocks: node, trigger, workflow, credential, and OAuth2.

Why learn:

Lets you read what the AI built and discuss adjustments.

Key concepts:

The trigger starts the flow; credentials connect apps; OAuth2 authorizes.

What it is:

The n8n AI nodes: AI agent node, text classifier, code node, and IF node.

Why learn:

Are the building blocks of practical projects (support, classification, branches).

Key concepts:

Classify before acting; branch with IF; custom logic in the code node.

What it is:

Data/RAG terms: vector store, embeddings, document loader, and text splitter.

Why learn:

Are the foundation for AI to answer based on your documents.

Key concepts:

Index docs; search by meaning; split text into chunks.

📖 Essential Glossary — Part 2 (Agentic, RAG, Deploy, Security)

The second half of the glossary: agentic terms (WAT, self-improvement loop, agentic gap), RAG, versioning/cloud, hosted execution, app/frontend, and security & payment.

Module progress 0% · 0 of 6
What it is:

Agentic terms: agent, agentic, WAT (Workflows/Agent/Tools), self-improvement loop, and agentic gap.

Why learn:

Are the backbone of tracks 2 and 4.

Key concepts:

Decides on its own; self-heals; what's missing in production (gap).

What it is:

RAG (retrieval-augmented generation): knowledge base, retrieval, and confidence score.

Why learn:

It's how AI responds based on your data, without making things up.

Key concepts:

Search before responding; knowledge base; score confidence.

What it is:

Git/cloud basics: GitHub, repo, commit, push, and the gh CLI.

Why learn:

It’s where the code lives in the cloud and where deploy pulls from.

Key concepts:

Snapshot (commit); send (push); history/rollback.

What it is:

Production terms: Trigger.dev, cron, webhook, payload, dev vs prod, trace, retry, and alert.

Why learn:

Are the vocabulary of track 6 (hosting and reliability).

Key concepts:

Schedule vs. event; trace = step by step; retry vs. bug.

What it is:

The app model: frontend, backend, request/response cycle, Vercel, and env var.

Why learn:

Fundamentals for building interfaces that other people use.

Key concepts:

What the user sees vs. the server; request → response; secret in an env var.

What it is:

Security/monetization terms: Supabase, RLS, migration, Stripe, checkout, price ID, webhook secret, and freemium.

Why learn:

It’s what turns an open, risky app into a secure, paid product.

Key concepts:

Isolate data (RLS); database migration; checkout; freemium model.
